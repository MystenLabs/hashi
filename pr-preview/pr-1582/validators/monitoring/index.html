Monitoring and Alerts

Prometheus endpoint​

The node serves metrics at http://127.0.0.1:9180/metrics (configurable through metrics-http-address).

curl -s http://localhost:9180/metrics | grep hashi_

Metrics push​

Configure the node to push its metrics to the Mysten-operated collection endpoint for your network. The Hashi team relies on this for fleet-wide visibility on both networks, so every operator must include this section in the validator config:

  • Mainnet
  • Testnet
[metrics-push]
push-url = "https://metrics-proxy.mainnet.sui.io:8443/publish/metrics"
# How often to push (default: 60)
# push-interval-seconds = 60
[metrics-push]
push-url = "https://metrics-proxy.testnet.sui.io:8443/publish/metrics"
# How often to push (default: 60)
# push-interval-seconds = 60

Notes:

  • [metrics-push] is a TOML table: keep it at the end of the config file with the other sections, per the field-order note in Validator config.
  • You need no extra credentials. The push authenticates with your existing tls-private-key as a TLS client identity, verified against the TLS public key you registered onchain. The endpoint accepts pushes only from committee members, so until your node is seated (at launch, or at a later committee formation if you register afterwards) the endpoint refuses each push and the node logs unable to push metrics. That is expected.
  • The node does not fail without this section; it silently starts without the push task. Check your config if the team reports not seeing your metrics.

hashi_mpc_unguarded_spawn_blocking_totalcounterMPC work started outside the per-peer budget. Healthy: 0.
hashi_peer_first_seen_timestamp_secondsgaugeWhen this node first saw each peer since start. The peer alerts need it.
hashi_withdrawal_signing_refused_totalcounterWithdrawal signing and construction calls this node refused as committee, cap, busy or shape, by peer, and deposit confirmation, withdrawal approval, withdrawal confirmation and guardian request calls refused as committee. While Hashi is paused, each of these calls and each partial-signature request counts as paused. Healthy: shape flat, and busy too while the leader has a guardian. committee moves at epoch changes, cap is noisy, and paused rises briefly when a pause starts and ends.
hashi_peer_bitcoind_places_in_use, hashi_peer_bitcoind_calls_in_use, hashi_peer_bitcoind_discoveries_in_usegaugePlaces in this node's Bitcoin monitor held by each peer's deposit confirmation, construction and withdrawal confirmation requests (at most 192; a deposit discovery keeps its starter's place until it ends), the Bitcoin Core RPCs running for them (at most 16), and their deposit discoveries (at most 64). A flat 16 calls means the peer keeps every call busy: a member doing it on purpose, or the leader at a node whose bitcoind is slow; compare across nodes.
hashi_peer_bitcoind_requests_shed_totalcounterRequests refused because they got no place in this node's Bitcoin monitor within 45 s of their arrival, by peer. Healthy: flat; an honest leader gives up after 40 s.
hashi_peer_bitcoind_discoveries_refused_totalcounterDeposit confirmations refused because the peer already had 64 deposit discoveries running here. An honest leader reaches this in a burst of new deposits when this node's discoveries are slow, so it is not alerted.
hashi_kyoto_block_fetch_secondshistogramTime from asking Kyoto for a block to its answer, queue wait included, for block scans and deposit discoveries. A discovery's fetch is recorded when Kyoto answers or drops it, even after the discovery has timed out; a block scan's fetch abandoned at a timeout, reorg or restart is recorded when it is abandoned.
hashi_peer_bridge_work_seconds_totalcounterSeconds this node spent validating and signing each peer's withdrawal signing and construction requests, at most two at a time per peer. A rate near 2 over an hour means the peer keeps both busy, which an honest leader with a guardian does not unless your node is much slower than its peers, or another member's bad partial signatures are slowing aggregation (hashi_mpc_partial_sig_mismatch_total names that member when the blame is certain).
hashi_peer_mpc_lock_seconds_totalcounterSeconds each peer's SendMessages and Complain requests held this node's MPC write lock. Those requests take it one at a time across all peers, so a rate near 1 means the peer keeps it busy, which slows this node's own presigning and key rotation; compare across nodes.
hashi_peer_store_reads_maxgaugeEach requester's peak store-reading RetrieveMessages since start, against the limit of 4. An honest requester rarely holds more than two.
hashi_mpc_peer_refusals_totalcounterMPC requests (side="request") and responses (side="response") this node refused from each peer. Healthy: flat. hashi_mpc_peer_refusals_first_timestamp_seconds dates each series' first refusal, for the alerts.
hashi_mpc_refusals_totalcounterThe same refusals by side, handler, protocol and reason, for finding which check refused.
hashi_mpc_stored_rows_refused_totalcounterThis node's own stored MPC rows refused as unusable, by protocol and phase (load, read or serve). Healthy: 0. hashi_mpc_stored_rows_refused_first_timestamp_seconds dates each series' first refusal.
hashi_mpc_committee_membersgaugeMembers of the committee of the installed MPC manager. The at-once refusal alert compares against it.

Not in the 1.0 releases. hashi_withdrawal_bitcoin_check_script_failures_total and hashi_withdrawal_bitcoin_check_blind come with the testmempoolaccept withdrawal check, which testnet-1.0 and mainnet-1.0 do not have. On 1.0 both metrics are absent and their alerts in Suggested alerts never fire.

Also not in the 1.0 releases. Of the hashi_peer_* and hashi_mpc_unguarded_* metrics, 1.0 has only hashi_peer_requests_shed_total, so there the alerts miss a shed in a series' first sample. On 1.0, hashi_withdrawal_signing_refused_total counts only committee and cap, so the pause alert never fires there, and hashi_kyoto_block_fetch_seconds is absent. The MPC refusal metrics and hashi_mpc_committee_members are absent on 1.0, so their alerts never fire there.

Is hashi_kyoto_connected_peers = 1 healthy? Yes. The embedded light client runs in whitelist-only mode and connects only to the peers listed in bitcoin-trusted-peers (see Bitcoin node), so it never discovers peers through DNS seeds or address gossip. It keeps a single peer connection, so 1 is the healthy value however many trusted peers you list; extra entries are failover candidates, not extra connections. What matters is that the value is non-zero and stable: the alert in Suggested alerts fires on == 0, not on a low count.

Checking committee membership and status. Query committee state through the CLI (note the flag placement from Global options):

hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml list            # all members for the current epoch
hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml view 0x<addr>   # one validator's details
hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml epoch           # current epoch info

See Committee for what membership means and how Hashi derives the member set from the Sui validator set.

The CLI is the status of record for committee membership. These pages do not link a public explorer view for Hashi committee state, because the team has confirmed none; do not rely on a third-party dashboard to determine whether your node is in the committee.

Suggested alerts​

ConditionSeverityAction
hashi_reconfig_in_progress == 1 for > 10 minCriticalCheck logs for DKG or rotation errors. Nodes abort and restart it themselves once it overruns its Sui epoch; hashi committee abort-reconfig is the manual fallback (see Stuck reconfigurations).
increase(hashi_reconfig_aborted_total[1h]) > 0WarningA reconfiguration stalled for a full Sui epoch and the nodes aborted it; the replacement starts on its own. Find out why the first one stalled (offline stake, bad endpoints or keys, partitions).
hashi_presig_pool_remaining == 0 for > 10 min on a committee memberCriticalNode cannot sign. Check reconfig status and MPC logs. The gauge also reads 0 right after a restart and on a node that hasn't been in the committee since it started; a node that has left keeps its last value.
hashi_kyoto_synced == 0 for > 5 minWarningLight client lost sync. Check Bitcoin RPC and P2P connectivity.
hashi_kyoto_connected_peers == 0 for > 5 minWarningNo Bitcoin P2P peers. Check bitcoin-trusted-peers and reachability. Each light-client restart briefly resets it to 0.
hashi_mpc_sign_failures_total increasingWarningInvestigate MPC protocol errors in logs.
hashi_sui_balance < 1e9 and hashi_sui_balance >= 0 (below 1 SUI)WarningGas wallet running low; the node stops landing Sui transactions when it empties. Top up the address named in the alert's labels. (-1 means not yet sampled, not empty.)
hashi_withdrawal_bitcoin_check_script_failures_total increasingCriticalYour bitcoind rejected a signed withdrawal. Tell the operator channel; if only you see it, check your bitcoind.
hashi_withdrawal_bitcoin_check_blind == 1 for > 10 minWarningYour node cannot check withdrawals. Allow testmempoolaccept; see the error log.
A per-peer shed counter from Key metrics, or a busy or shape withdrawal signing or construction refusal, counted in the last hour (see monitoring/validator-alerts.example.yml)WarningFor a shed counter, the peer might be misbehaving, or your node might be slow or have its limits set too low: rule your node out first (hashi_request_latency). A busy or shape refusal points at the peer, however slow your node is: an honest leader causes neither unless it runs without a guardian (busy). Then look the peer up with hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml view <peer>.
3 or more peers hit the request count or MPC work limits within 10 minCriticalUsually your node is slow, not the peers, though colluding members can also cause it. Check hashi_request_latency and tell the operator channel.
A peer asked your node to sign more than about 3 min into a pause (see monitoring/validator-alerts.example.yml)WarningUnless a Sui view lags or a 1.0 node polls during a rollout, the peer is ignoring the pause. Look it up with hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml view <peer> and tell the operator channel.
hashi_mpc_unguarded_spawn_blocking_total increasingWarningA node bug. Report it to the hashi team with your version.
A peer's MPC requests or responses refused in the last hour (see monitoring/validator-alerts.example.yml)WarningOne peer usually means it is faulty or runs a broken build. A response refused for a certificate hash mismatch (reason="hash" in hashi_mpc_refusals_total) can also come from an honest signer that lost its stored copy. Look the peer up with hashi committee -c hashi-cli.toml view <peer>.
MPC messages from 3 or more peers, and more than half of the committee, refused within 10 minCriticalUsually your node's checks and its peers' messages disagree. Compare your build with the refused peers' first, from each node's GetServiceInfo reply: in a staged rollout, nodes on different builds can refuse each other's messages. Tell the operator channel.
Your node refused one of its own stored MPC rows in the last hourWarningIts store is damaged or, when several nodes see this right after an upgrade, the new build refuses what an earlier one accepted. A rotation that needs the row can fail. Tell the operator channel with your version and the alert's phase.
up == 0 (Prometheus target down)CriticalNode is down. Restart and check logs.
